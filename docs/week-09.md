# Week 9

**Goal this week:** Build the frame that holds the conveyor at a 30 degree slope and attach it to the PVC hulls.

## What we did

- Planned the frame: two horizontal bars to hold the top and bottom ends of the conveyor, which is slanted at 30 degrees.
- Cut 1-inch hollow metal pipes for the horizontal bars with a hand-held motorised cut-off saw. Drilled holes in them so a U-clamp could hold the 110 mm PVC hull pipes on either side.
- For the double side frame of the conveyor, used the bottom part of a flex banner stand. It was light and had the right shape, but it is aluminium, so it cannot be welded to the steel pipes.
- Plan B: stack 1-inch steel pipes to build a frame about 7 cm (3 inches) thick. I built this in-house with help from the Tinkerspace staff member who is trusted with the power tools.
- Made the angled piece that holds the conveyor at 30 degrees on the horizontal frame. I measured the angle with a protractor, drew a line on the metal, and cut along it with the hand saw.
- Learned to use the power saw without damaging the tool or the piece, and the safety precautions needed to avoid injury.
- Worked through the conveyor sizing, parts list and a draft of the ESP32 control firmware with Claude. None of this was tested on hardware.

## Problems and blockers

- Aluminium cannot be welded to the steel pipes, so the banner-stand frame could not be joined to the steel frame.
- Cutting a precise 30 degree piece by hand was very slow and difficult as a beginner.
- Time: this frame work took far longer than planned and used up most of the week.

## Decisions

- Build the conveyor frame from stacked 1-inch steel pipes, instead of joining it to the aluminium banner-stand frame.

## Next week
