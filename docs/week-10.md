# Week 10

**Goal this week:** Get a prototype ready for demo day.

## What we did

- Bought 4 ball bearings for the rollers. The bearings have rubber seals that keep debris out of the balls. Welding would melt these seals and block the balls, so welding the bearings to the metal pipes was not possible.
- The stacked steel pipes turned out too heavy. Carrying that weight would have needed larger hulls, so I went back to the aluminium frame for the demo.
- Cut the aluminium frame pieces to the size of the bearings, so each bearing sits partly inside the frame, and fixed all four with a glue gun.
- Made the two rollers (top and bottom of the conveyor) from PVC pipe, about 40 cm wide. The end caps are drilled so that smaller, shorter pipes pass through them and into the bearings.
- Used a mesh net as the belt. Got a timing belt from a nearby workshop and stuck pieces under the mesh and around one of the rollers, to grip the belt.
- Tied the conveyor assembly to horizontal PVC pipes, which replaced the heavy metal pipes.
- Chose paddles with long blades for propulsion, planned one on each side.
- The original demo date (11/09) was moved to 13/09. Most projects in the cohort were unfinished, so Shan asked Circuit Digest to extend the deadline to 20/09. The demo took place on 20/09/2026.

## Problems and blockers

- **Belt friction:** the belt moves with difficulty. My diagnosis is that the adhesive used to stick the timing belt to the mesh made it stiff, and that I attached it to the roller unevenly.
- **Time:** I underestimated the deadline and did not manage my time well. At the demo I had only the body with the two PVC hulls and the conveyor with its belt. No propulsion, electronics or collection bin was fitted, and the prototype was not ready to touch water.
- **Algae and plant:** the type of algae is still not confirmed. I could not do a pull test to check whether it is rooted, because the water is too far below the banks to reach the weed.
- **Fish safety:** a scholar advised starting by removing the floating weeds. Since a student group's experiment was once followed by a fish die-off, I still need to ask whether even this is safe for the fish, and how oxygen should be monitored.

## Decisions

- **PVC over steel for the cross pipes, aluminium over steel for the side frames:** mainly weight.
- **Paddles instead of propellers:** the Orange motors cannot be submerged. A motor on board with the propeller under water at an angle would need universal joints, which were not readily available and too complicated for the deadline.
- **Orange motors:** the space already had them. The plan was one or two for the conveyor roller and one for each paddle.
